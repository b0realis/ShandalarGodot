The world

world.json is a recipe, not a map. The game cuts an island from its own 1997 terrain generator and lays your places out with the adventure’s dice: the same island every time for the same campaign, a different one for each new campaign.

How an island is made

The island is made the first time the hero sails to it, from the campaign’s world seed and your recipe. Its terrain comes first, then the dice are drawn in this order, which is the island:

  1. Terrain. The game runs its world generator for the campaign’s seed XOR the manifest’s region.salt, reads every step-th tile of that world into a side × side square and puts the square at offset on the 64-tile map. The largest connected land is kept; any other land becomes sea, so every place is reachable on foot. The island’s dice are the generated world’s, XOR dice_salt.
  2. The castle. Its aim is castle.from plus a draw under castle.span, x then y. It stands on the land tile nearest the aim.
  3. The port. Aimed the same way from port.from and port.span, on the nearest shore tile (a land tile touching the sea) at least spacing tiles from the castle.
  4. The turn. One draw under towns.turns turns every town aim together by that many quarter turns.
  5. The towns, in list order. Each aim is towns.center plus the town’s turned aim plus a draw under spread less half of it, x then y. The town takes the nearest land tile at least spacing from everything placed, and that tile takes the town’s terrain, which is its color.
  6. The places (places.list), each on a random tile at least places.gap from everything placed (a coastal one on the shore). When nothing fits, the gap shrinks by half a tile until something does.
  7. The villages. Their number is drawn from villages.count (least, most). Village i (from 0) takes slot (i + 1) × slot_step and the i-th name of villages.names; the first one prefers tiles that match first_rule.
  8. Trade. Each town and place gets one trade category from trade, drawn without repeats (none once the list runs out). Then world_magic.offers world magics are dealt to them.
  9. Roads join the places when roads is true.
  10. The dungeon. One of the generated world’s dungeons, number dungeon.from plus a draw under dungeon.choices, with its door on a tile with no site and no road, at least dungeon.gap from everything placed. It is named dungeon.name.

A span of 0 or 1 draws nothing. Because every draw follows the one before it, adding a place or reordering the towns moves everything drawn after it. Names and objectives use no dice.

The recipe’s fields

Every field but generator, towns and dungeon has a default, and the defaults are the Refuge’s own numbers. The full list, with limits, is on the Reference page; the parts an author changes most:

A town’s slot is its identity on the island: quests name their giver by slot, errands_off lists slots, and an objective’s exclude and prefer are slots. There are 128 slots; towns and places take 1 to 127, villages take multiples of slot_step, and no slot may be used twice. terrain is one of the land codes the generator makes:

  • town
  • city

Objectives

An objective is a named rule that picks a site once the island exists: the first site the rule matches, or its prefer slot when that one matches; -1 when none does. It uses no dice and moves nothing. It is chosen when the hero first lands and kept in the save. A quest step aims at one as {"objective": "channel", "else": "port"}: the else target is used when nothing matched.

coastal, road
The site touches the sea; the site stands on a road.
near_terrain, radius
A tile of that terrain within radius tiles (default 2).
types, exclude
Only these kinds of site; never these slots.
prefer
The slot chosen when it matches (the Refuge’s channel prefers Svyelun’s Temple, slot 22).

The port and the castle

places.json describes the island’s two markers, port and castle; both are required. Each has a name, a picture for its pane, an optional map sprite and banner (Art and music), and a list of panes. The port also has a mainland_name: the title of its pane at the mainland’s anchorage, where the map labels the ship Ship: region title.

When the hero steps on a marker, the first pane whose when conditions all hold is shown, so put the most particular panes first and leave the last without when. A pane has:

  • lines: a text key, {"text": key, "when": [...]}, or {"show": ...} for one of
    • news
    • pending
    • deck_problem
    : the region’s news, the waiting-cards line, the reason a duel may not begin yet.
  • actions, buttons, each one of
    • sail
    • leave
    • boss
    • rewards
    with a label and its own when. sail is the voyage (on the mainland only while the plugin is on for the campaign; on the island always), leave closes the pane, boss starts the finale’s duel when its conditions hold, rewards adds the two reward buttons (choose cards, collect waiting cards).
  • title, subtitle and picture override the marker’s for this pane.

The region’s other switches

The palette

The manifest’s region.palette colors the island’s ground, trees and water; it never changes the terrain itself. It is a built-in name (shandalar, the skin’s own colors, or last_refuge, the Refuge’s drier greens) or an object of your own:

"palette": {
  "name": "Ashen Isle",
  "grass": [0.11, 0.55, 1.1, 0.9],
  "foliage": [0.2, 0.7, 1.0, 0.7],
  "water": [0.6, 1.0, 0.95, 0.8],
  "plain": {"2": "#858855", "6": "#c6af78"},
  "tints": {"roads": {"tint": "#b09070", "strength": 0.3}}
}

grass, foliage and water are four numbers: the target hue (0 to 1), a saturation and a brightness multiplier (0 to 4), and the strength (0 to 1); leave one out to keep the skin’s colors. plain maps terrain codes (as text, "0" to "15") to #rrggbb colors for the minimap and the plain map. tints gives the ground, objects or roads layer a tint and a strength (0 to 1). The game registers the object as the palette plugin:id; a save that names it while the plugin is gone draws the island in the skin’s colors.

Mana links and music

region.mana_links (default false) says whether the island’s places can be mana-linked as the mainland’s are. world.json’s optional music names an OGG file of the plugin, played while the hero walks the island (Art and music).
