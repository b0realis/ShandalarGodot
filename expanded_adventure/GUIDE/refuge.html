The Refuge walkthrough

The Last Refuge is the game’s one plugin: a finished island made as a game-design and learning demo. This page reads it file by file, quoting the files as they are.

Where to find it

The game folder’s expanded_adventure/Fallen_Empires_Refuge_prototype.zip. Unzip a copy anywhere to read it, or to start your own from it under a new id: then delete files from your unzipped copy’s plugin.json, or just export, which ignores it. Its island is original fiction, not official Magic canon and not new official content; its five communities draw on the peoples of the Fallen Empires card set.

plugin.json

  • id is refuge: the island’s name in every save, the start of its picture keys (refuge_harbor) and of every button the game makes for it.
  • requires names Pack 2, Fallen Empires: its cards fill every deck and reward, and the finale asks for a deck of them. Ticking the plugin ticks the pack.
  • region.salt picks the island’s own terrain from the campaign’s seed; palette is the built-in last_refuge, drier, yellower greens and its own water; mana_links is false, so its places cannot be mana-linked.
  • The game’s source folder, quoted here, has no files list; export writes one into the zip, so the shipped zip’s plugin.json has it. In an unzipped copy you edit, delete files, or just export, which ignores it and writes a fresh one.

world.json

  • The island is 32 tiles square at offset 16, every second tile of the generated world (step 2). layout_version 4 is saved with every island the recipe makes. The same recipe and seed always make the same island; the game’s tests hold this recipe to 70 islands, seed by seed.
  • The five towns are the communities that give the five quests, slots 1 to 5, each on the terrain of its color: Leitbur’s Rest white, Vodalian Haven blue, the Freehold of the Hand black, Dwarven Hearth red, Havenwood green. turns: 4 turns their arrangement a different way for different campaigns.
  • The six places carry the mainland’s services; Svyelun’s Temple (slot 22) stands on the coast, where the harbor quest’s channel prefers it.
  • Six to nine villages, the first near red terrain (5) within two tiles, where the Mountain Road’s raider objective prefers to find its village.
  • objectives: the channel is a coastal town or city that is not one of the five communities, Svyelun’s Temple if it qualifies; the raider is a village on a road near red terrain, the first village if it qualifies.

places.json

  • The port’s panes are tried in order. On the island: the ship home. On the mainland, once the island is free: the welcome back. Otherwise: the captain’s story, the danger and the gate, which is what a hero reads first.
  • The castle’s free pane shows the reward buttons and the way out; its other pane counts pledges and the deck gate, shows why a duel cannot begin, and offers the challenge.
  • The castle flies a gold pennant (e0bc60) once liberated is set, at the anchor that suits its sprite.

A Harbor for Both Peoples: a go step aimed at an objective

The channel survey goes to the channel objective, or the port when the island has no qualifying site. Its line is said at a town target.

No More Masters: a duel alone

The Mountain Road: a duel away from the giver

After the petition, the duel waits at the raider village. The approach puts its button and line in that village’s pane, and opens the quest there.

The pledge, the finale and the rest

All five end with the "pledge" completion: one reward choice, 40 gold, 8 food, the pennants redrawn, the journal’s entry, and waiting cards claimed. The finale needs every pledge, the island not yet free, and a main deck whose nonland cards are at least half Fallen Empires:

errands_off lists slots 0 to 5, so the five communities offer no ordinary errands; talk gives the wise men their news, applause and seven tales, the roaming creatures their words, and the Vaults of the First Crossing theirs:

foes.json

Six foes: one per quest at 26 to 30 life, and the Lord of the Ebon Crown at 36, each deck one color of Fallen Empires cards with a few base-game cards and its basic lands. The roaming creatures take the foes’ decks by their own color, from tier 12 up; one creature in three, by a fixed rule of the world, keeps its own:

rewards.json

Each pledge owes one choice of six cards in one color; the hero may take the same color again to build one deck.
