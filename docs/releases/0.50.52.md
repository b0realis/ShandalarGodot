# Shandalar 0.50.52

**Magic Public Notation (MPN) — a proposal to the community.** Every duel
you play can now be saved as a public history of the game: what both
players could see, never a hidden hand. MPN is a proposed open format for
such records, independent of this game — reading one needs a JSON parser
and the specification's patch rules, not a rules engine or a card
database. The specification, a player guide and two example files are on
the project's GitHub page, in the `docs` folder (`mpn-specification.md`,
`mpn-v0.1.md`, `mpn-examples`). It is a draft and not an official Magic
standard: makers of engines, viewers, tournament tools and archives are
warmly invited to read it, try it and tell us what it needs, in an issue
on the project's GitHub page with "MPN" in the title.

Fixes on top of 0.50.34, the published release.

- **The card shop.** In a village, town or city, Buy Cards opens its shop
  on the 1997 shop's own ground, "Cards for Sale": four cards in a
  village, five in a town, six in a city (one more with Haggler's Coin),
  drawn as the 1997 game drew them — the colors of the land the town
  stands on, a basic land among them, common, uncommon and rare places in
  turn, never more copies than your deck may hold — and kept: a card you
  buy leaves its place empty until a new one comes, days later. Rest the
  pointer on a card to see it large, click it, Buy. Your adventure keeps
  every town's shop.
- **Sell your cards.** The deck builder opened from a town's Edit
  deck/Sell cards has Sell: one copy of the card on show, never one the
  deck on the surface holds, for the town's price.
- **The 1997 prices.** A card's price is the 1997 game's own formula — its
  type, cost, power and toughness, keywords and abilities, its rarity —
  three times in a village, four in a town, five in a city, more for a
  card of another color. Build your skin again with
  `tools/import_original.py` and the prices are exactly the 1997 game's,
  read from your copy of it (every card it lists, however it spells the
  name); without it, and for every card the 1997 game never had, the same
  formula reads the card itself, its abilities included.
- **Trade for cards.** Every town and city trades one kind of card — a
  color's, or a type's, such as any creatures — for amulets of a color of
  its land: "Trade red amulet for Any creatures" stands where the wise man
  is while you hold one. The trader lists every card of that kind with
  how many you own, with color and type chips and a name to look for; a
  card costs its rarity in amulets, basic lands four for one. After a
  trade the town waits some days before the next.
- **The card seller's hints.** "Buy Cards/Card Hints": the seller points
  out a card on offer that works with one you own, and what the two do
  together, in the 1997 game's words from your copy of it.
- **Lairs.** Lairs lie hidden about the land and show, in the 1997 game's
  own pictures, only when you come beside one: walk onto it and it opens.
  Two times in three it holds a card of its color; otherwise one of
  fourteen places — a Ruined Tower's clue, riddles on cards at
  Loremaster's Tower and the Pyramids of Aska-tet, the Thieves Hideout,
  the Lost City of El'Akram, the Gemcutter's Guild's amulets, the Nomad's
  Bazaar (any card for gold), the Diamond Mine (any card for amulets) and
  the Oasis of Muldoon (a card for five extra life in your next duel). The
  lairs that hold duels wait for the duels. Sometimes a rare waits as
  well, given as you leave.
- **Read a card full screen.** Right-click a card in a shop, the card
  trader or a lair (on a touch screen, press and hold) and it opens full
  screen, as in a duel. The options switch it: "Large card on
  right-click".

## From 0.50.51

- **Cast the World Magics.** On the world map, click a World Magic's icon
  or press its number to cast it: Conjurer's Will (1), Leap of Fate (2),
  Quickening (3), Staff of Thunder (4) and Sword of Resistance (5), the
  number shown on the icon while it can be cast. Leap of Fate sets you
  down somewhere else in Shandalar, wherever the dice say; Quickening gives
  a burst of speed, its icon lit while it lasts. As in 1997, each risks an
  amulet of its color: an Apprentice loses it one time in four, a Wizard
  every time. A World Magic with nothing to work on says so and risks
  nothing. Each icon's tip now gives its 1997 words.
- **Panes you can move.** A small map in the corner shows the land round
  your hero (N shows or hides it; click on it to walk there). A quest pane
  shows what calls you, in the 1997 game's words: the quest, where to go
  and the days left, a city under siege, a bonus for your next duel and
  the cities each wizard has tapped toward the Spell of Dominion. Guide
  arrows at the screen's edge point to the quest's town and to a city under
  siege. The gate on the panel (F4) opens the dungeon and castle clues:
  treasure, creatures, rules and where each lies. The small map, the quest
  pane, the clues, your hero's journal and the keys float in panes with
  round corners; drag one anywhere and it stays there, next time too.
  Quests, sieges and clues are found once the towns and the encounters
  come.
- **Your first deck and the deck builder.** A new hero sets out with a
  first deck dealt by the 1997 game's rules: by difficulty, your color's
  basic lands, spells and creatures and one rare, then more colors, from
  the base cards at the rarity of their first printing — 36 cards for an
  Apprentice up to 45 for a Wizard. The spellbook (F1) now opens the deck
  builder over the world: the cards along the bottom are your own, those
  not yet in the deck on show, and no deck holds more copies than you own.
  Deck1, Deck2 and Deck3 are your three decks; the one on show when you
  press Done is the deck you duel with. The deck's name pane shows your
  gold, "GOLD: 200", as the 1997 builder's title did; click the name to
  rename the deck, and your adventure keeps it. Nothing from outside
  your cards: no Extras, loading, proxies, drafts or sideboard; Tome of
  Enlightenment eases the duplicate limit. Conjurer's Will opens it too.
- **Your hero's stats.** The faces (F5) open a pane of your hero's stats
  in the 1997 stats screen's own captions: your name, what you carry, your World Magics, the wizards' castles conquered and the
  attempts at each, cities saved and recovered, the quest record, duels
  won and lost, dungeon treasures, your record against each creature and
  your days on the road; a Wizards tab with each wizard's cities tapped
  toward Dominion, and the Journal, newest first.
- **Touch and touchpads.** On the world map a pinch zooms, on a
  touchscreen or a laptop's touchpad, and a touchpad's two-finger slide
  looks around. Big buttons, on by themselves where the touch controls
  are on and switchable in Options, make the panel and its options large
  enough for a finger; the panel's row scrolls with a swipe or with red
  arrows at its two ends. The world's screens were checked from 640 × 400
  up to 3440 × 1440, Steam Deck's 1280 × 800 included, and in a tall
  window; the quest pane now moves out of the way when the panel goes to
  the bottom. A long journal scrolls, with a gold bar.
- **Visit the places of Shandalar.** Walk to a village, town or city, a
  wizard's castle, a lair or a dungeon you have clues to, and it opens in
  a pane in the middle of the screen with the 1997 game's own picture of
  the place — the village's farmhouse, the city at dusk, each wizard's
  castle, the lairs (the blue one's sunken ship) — its name and the 1997
  words and choices. In a town you can open your deck and buy 10 food
  (15, 20 or 25 gold, as in 1997); a castle asks whether you dare to
  enter. The card shop, the wise man, the duels and the dungeons come
  next. Build your skin again with `tools/import_original.py` to bring the
  pictures in.
- **The pointer shows the place.** Over a place you can see and visit,
  the pointer becomes it: a little house for a village, two houses for a
  town, a towered gate for a city, a castle, a beaten wizard's broken
  tower, a wizard's dome over a town a wizard rules, a skull over a dungeon, a
  cave over a lair; a gold arrow over the panel.
- **More options.** Switch the small map, the quest pane and the guide
  arrows on or off, set the pace of the world (as in 1997, brisk or swift),
  switch the music and the sound, or return to the main menu. Leaving the
  world, by Escape or from the options, saves the adventure first. Your
  hero's journal counts the days on the road.

## From 0.50.50

- **Walk the world of Shandalar.** Click where your hero should go: the
  way there is found around the water, and your hero walks it at the
  1997 game's own pace — plain land at an even step, forest, swamp and
  mountains slower (unless an Amulet of Swampwalk or a Dwarven Pick says
  otherwise), a road quicker, and slower still with no food left. A food
  is eaten every so often on the march. The pointer is a small walking
  man, and an open door over a town, village or castle you can see. The
  land your hero passes is seen, the view goes along with them, and the
  screen shows what they carry: gold, food and amulets.
- **Dress your hero.** On the Embark screen the figure that walks the map
  can be colored part by part — hair, skin, body, arms, pants and boots —
  each from its own colors, keeping the 1997 figure's own shading; 1997
  puts it back in the old game's colors, and Randomize dresses it too. The
  figure walks under the colors as you choose, and on the map in them; the
  red arrows now stand either side of the face.
- **The 1997 frame's icons float on one panel across the world.** Your
  gold, food, mana links and deck on little tiles of the old frame's
  glass, your five amulets, the twelve World Magics lit when they are
  yours, then the frame's round buttons — the spellbook for your deck, the
  scroll for the map, the faces for your hero's journal and stats — the
  painted book for the options and ? for the keys. The options save, load
  another adventure, move the panel to the bottom of the screen (it stays
  there) and exit the game. Your name and difficulty stand by the panel
  in your color of magic; rest the pointer on an icon and its name floats
  beside it. They come from your copy of the 1997 game (build your skin
  again to bring them in); without it each is its word.
- **Your adventure is saved.** The moment its world is made the
  adventure is saved under your hero's name: everything the 1997 game
  kept — gold, food, amulets, World Magics, the record and the clocks, and
  the world as it stands, the land you have seen included — in a plain
  text file you can read. Save / Load on the main menu lists the
  adventures saved, newest first, and Continue takes you back into one.

## From 0.50.48

- **Shandalar: embark on an adventure.** Shandalar on the main menu opens
  Embark to the world of Shandalar, where a new adventure's hero is made
  on one screen: a face and a name, the figure that walks the world map
  (male, female, or either), the color of your magic with the amulet and
  world magic it starts you with, your playmat, the card packs that join
  as additional challenges (from Pack 2 on; the base cards are always
  present), the difficulty (a training, easy, normal or hard game) with
  what you start with, and the world's seed: every world is made at random
  from one, as in 1997, the seed comes filled in from the clock as the
  1997 game seeded its dice, written as the time of day (14:35:07 makes
  143507), and typing a number or a word as the seed makes the same world
  again. Randomize draws every choice at random, a magical (sometimes
  funny) name included, for you to change before you set out; Adventure
  keeps your hero and sets out.
- **The world of Shandalar.** Every adventure makes its own world, exactly
  as the 1997 game made its worlds: the same island of five colors, the
  villages, towns and cities, the roads, the World Magics, the wizards'
  castles and the dungeons, from the same seed. It fills the whole screen,
  drawn as the original drew it, at twice its size, and only the land your
  hero has seen is shown, the rest dark, as in 1997: look around with the
  arrow keys or by dragging, zoom with the mouse wheel, and press M for
  the map of the world. Every seed makes a world: the few on which the
  1997 game hung for good while making it are made again, and while the
  world is made an hourglass runs in the screen's bottom strip beside the
  words "Conjuration of Shandalar World" and a row of stars, until the
  world appears. Walking, the towns and the encounters come next.
- **New pictures from your copy of the 1997 game**: the Embark screen's
  dark ground (`Menu4.pic`) and the world's sprites. Build your skin again
  with `tools/mtg_assets.py` or `tools/import_original.py` to bring them
  in; without them the Embark screen is the same dark, plain, and the
  world is drawn in plain colors.

## From 0.50.45

- **Ante cards need ante.** Contract from Below, Darkpact, Demonic
  Attorney, Jeweled Bird, Bronze Tablet, Rebirth, Tempest Efreet,
  Timmerian Fiends and Amulet of Quoz say *"Remove this card from your
  deck before playing if you're not playing for ante."* In a duel not
  played for ante they can no longer be cast or used, and the game tells
  you: *"Ante must be on for Contract from Below"*. Nothing goes to the
  ante, so the opening window shows only card backs there. Decks that hold
  these cards are unchanged; the computer discards them first and
  mulligans hands that rely on them. Duels played for ante work as before.
- **Fireball divides among the targets you chose.** X is split evenly,
  rounded down, among all the targets chosen when Fireball is cast. A
  target that is gone when it resolves loses its share; the others take
  no more.
- **The 1997 legend rule: the newcomer goes.** A Vesuvan Doppelganger that
  becomes a copy of a legend already in play is the one put into the
  graveyard, whichever entered first: "first in time" counts from when
  each became that legend. The same for a Clone, Volrath's Shapeshifter
  and a face-down legend turned face up. The computer's Doppelganger no
  longer copies a legend.
- **Illusionary Mask.** A masked creature now turns face up when it
  attacks, regenerates or is tapped for an ability or for mana, and a
  masked creature in combat deals its real damage. Masking is now a "you
  may". Plague Rats, Plaguebearer, Apocalypse Chime, Echo Chamber, Crown
  of the Ages and the computer no longer read the card under the Mask.
- **Banding.** Coils of the Medusa, Elvish Berserker, Wall of Nets, Abu
  Ja'far, The Wretched, Spitting Slug, Wall of Caltrops and Feint now count
  every creature blocking or blocked through a band. A creature told to
  block a banded attacker (Crashing Boars, Lure) may block any creature in
  that band.
- **Creatures that left combat.** A creature that regenerates or is
  removed from combat is no longer destroyed, paralysed, shrunk or exiled
  at end of combat by the creature that blocked it (Kjeldoran Frostbeast,
  Dread Wight, Greater Werewolf, Wall of Nets), and Goblin Snowman, Tinder
  Wall, Wall of Corpses, Whip Vine and Sworn Defender no longer target it.
  False Orders, Ydwen Efreet and Imprison no longer let through an
  attacker that two creatures blocked.
- **More card fixes.**
  - **Glyph of Doom** now destroys the blocked creatures even when its
    Wall died in the fight.
  - **Stasis** really skips untap steps.
  - **Island Sanctuary** ends at your upkeep, even when your untap step is
    skipped.
  - **Equinox** reads what a spell really targets, Volcanic Eruption
    included.
  - **Enchantment Alteration** and **Kudzu** skip what can't be
    enchanted.
  - **Drain Power** aimed at yourself keeps your own mana.
  - **Berserk** and **Blood Frenzy** can't be cast after first-strike
    damage.
  - **The Fallen** no longer hits its own controller.
  - **Clockwork Beast** and **Clockwork Avian** lose counters only for
    combats they fought in.
  - **Tetravus**'s tokens are Tetravites.
  - **Titania's Song** sizes its creatures in timestamp order with
    Humility.
  - **Regeneration** and **The Brute** no longer leave a free shield
    behind.
  - **Vesuvan Doppelganger** keeps its own blue, not a colour another card
    painted on it, and a Clone of its new shape is blue too.
  - **Consecrate Land** and **Anti-Magic Aura** now knock off Auras already
    attached.
  - **Disintegrate** exiles a token instead of letting it die.
  - **Seeds of Innocence** gives life for an artifact Forbidden Crypt
    exiles.
  - A stolen **Purgatory** returns cards to its controller.
  - **Dream Coat**, **Prismatic Lace** and **Shyft** let you choose several
    colours, also at a network table.
  - A cost lowered by another card (Basalt Monolith under Power Artifact)
    shows its real cost in messages and on the card.
- **The computer opponent.**
  - It casts card-draw instants such as Ancestral Recall before the
    end-of-turn discard instead of throwing them away.
  - It no longer casts Final Fortune on your turn.
  - Cold Storage no longer stores a creature from a spell that would not
    kill it, or into a Storage that spell destroys.
  - It no longer casts Reins of Power into its own blocker, and Corpse
    Dance no longer returns a creature that cannot attack.
  - It no longer phases a blocker out of the way of a trampler that then
    kills it.
  - It respects "can't be blocked by more than one creature" when it plans
    blocks.
  - It no longer pays Phelddagrif's or Urza's Engine's abilities for
    nothing.
  - It now protects its creatures from Pyrotechnics and other divided
    damage.
  - The stronger opponents no longer throw small creatures in front of big
    attackers while their life is high.
  - It no longer keeps a hand whose only mana is Mox Diamond.
  - It no longer gives your creatures landwalk with Part Water.
- **The duel screen.**
  - A question, the aiming cursor and the Concede question now close when
    the duel ends.
  - The Pause window stays on top of an open question.
  - Leaving during the opening no longer counts as "Play first" or a kept
    hand.
  - A phased-out face-down card is no longer revealed by a click.
  - The play/draw and mulligan questions answer to Return and Space.
  - Dragging an Aura moves the creature or land it enchants, and a slip on
    a card in the spell chain no longer moves a permanent.
  - Auras on a land in the front row are no longer cut off.
  - Piled cards honour Winter Orb and show "Is a target".
  - The big card stays clickable with a graveyard open beneath it.
  - Hotseat duels name the right seat in their library and draw lines.
  - Quitting a match from Pause while the opening splash runs now waits
    for your answer.
- **Elsewhere.**
  - Test hands' **Clear all** resets lives to Magic Battle's starting
    lives, and Magic Battle no longer slides 8 pixels sideways at
    1280x800.
  - Deck notes keep their blank lines and indents.
  - Card rarity follows each card's first printing in the Deck Builder's
    filters and stats and in Sealed and Booster Draft (Hurr Jackal was
    shown as rare).
  - Escape leaves Booster Draft.
  - Touch sliders work inside scrolling pages, the on-screen keyboard
    reaches its text fields with the D-pad, and a pad connected mid-press
    no longer sticks.
  - `duel_log.txt` is never left cut while it is trimmed.
  - A deck list stops at 100,000 cards, with a message, instead of growing
    without end.
  - A finished network duel no longer opens a question.
- **Game history.** Watch duel no longer breaks on a recording from
  another program, a damaged record is refused instead of shown with
  garbled text, and dragging the Past Games seek bar no longer freezes on
  long games.
- **Tools.** A failed skin or card-art zip build no longer replaces the
  earlier zip; the Manalink import names a frame it cannot read instead
  of stopping; a damaged movie no longer ends the 1997 import; the skin
  checker in a release reports pictures at the wrong size again; the Quest
  install script accepts "yes" at its questions.

## From 0.50.42

- **A spell aimed at a creature that left play and came back now
  fizzles.** A creature bounced and recast, or set free from Tawnos's
  Coffin, is a new creature: a Terror, a Lightning Bolt or a Prodigal
  Sorcerer's ping that was aimed at it before no longer hits it.
- **A card you cast from your opponent's library is yours while it is a
  spell.** With Grinning Totem, "target opponent" now means your opponent,
  and Reflect Damage or Eye for an Eye answer you, not the card's owner.
- **1997 rules: the upkeep and the draw are phases of their own.** Under
  the 1997 (Fifth Edition) rules, mana left in your pool at the end of the
  upkeep empties then, and burns with mana burn on, and a player below 1
  life at the end of the upkeep loses there, as the 1997 manual has it.
  The modern rules are unchanged.
- **Gem Bazaar keeps its colour** while the computer thinks about its
  moves.
- **Leaving a Gauntlet or match duel asks first.** In a Gauntlet, the
  duel's opening screen now offers **Quit Gauntlet**, and it and **Exit
  duel** ask before your run ends, instead of dropping you on Magic Battle.
  In a best-of match, **Reconfigure duel** and **Exit duel** ask before the
  match record is lost.
- **Test hands: only permanents go on the table.** The "On the table" list
  no longer offers instants and sorceries, and a saved test with one there
  says which card it is and where it can go instead.
- **The computer plays its combos better.** It no longer runs a mana loop
  for cards it is not going to cast, so it stops losing life to mana burn
  after a combo turn; a second Power Artifact goes on a second Basalt
  Monolith, and a second Ashnod's Altar no longer counts as progress.
- **The computer wastes fewer cards.** It no longer casts Shrieking Drake
  just to return the Drake itself; it returns a creature worth casting
  again, or keeps the Drake. Under mana burn it saves Mana Drain for a real
  threat when it could not use the mana. The hardest opponent casts its
  small creatures into a Goblin Bombardment or Orcish Mechanics that has
  nothing to sacrifice.
- **A card pack from another release says what is wrong.** Instead of
  "manifest mismatch", Card Packs now says when a pack needs a newer game,
  or is another version of the pack than this game expects, and which tool
  rebuilds it.
- **Choosing a new skin, card-art zip or card back keeps the old one safe**
  until the new file is completely written, so a full disk or a crash
  part-way no longer breaks it.
- **Hand-edited settings.** A stray number in the card-pack list, in the
  names of your saved tests or in the Deck Builder's Extras and Sealed
  settings no longer switches packs off or stops a list from showing.
- **The Web download's README no longer promises LAN play.** LAN play needs
  an installed build: a browser page cannot open the connections it uses.
  The offline Web packer now also refuses a threaded Web export, as its
  instructions say.
- **The Android guide explains how to add numbered card packs**, and that
  Options → Skin takes skins and card pictures only.

## From 0.50.41

- **Experimental Android ARM64.** A separate phone/tablet export recipe,
  signed-APK checks and plain/classic-skin packages. Android 10 or newer,
  a 64-bit OS and OpenGL ES 3 are the initial support target. Physical
  devices still need testing. Meta Quest remains a separate target;
  this does not add a Play Store release.
- **Optional offline Web packages.** A verified single-threaded Web ZIP
  can gain background caching without changing its engine or adding a
  start screen. The first load needs a connection; subsequent offline
  starts depend on the browser retaining the cached files. Updates wait
  for the running game to close, and incomplete downloads do not replace
  a working cache. Setup instructions stay in a text file.
- **Clearer Web setup.** HTTPS is required when playing from another
  device, including over a local network. HTTP on localhost works for
  same-device testing. Only threaded Web also needs isolation headers.

## From 0.50.39

- **Test hands.** Magic Battle has a new **Test hands** button, at the
  bottom of its settings under the ante and match settings. Choose
  the decks and the opponent as usual, then set up each side: the cards in
  hand (picked from the deck, dealt at random, or any card from your
  enabled packs), the cards on the table (tapped or untapped, and a
  creature summoning sick or ready), the graveyard, the top of the library
  and the life total; then whose turn it is and the step it starts in.
  **Go test!** drops you straight into that turn: no coin toss, no
  mulligan. **Restart test** plays the same test again with the same
  shuffle, **Edit test** takes you back to change it, and tests can be
  saved by name. A test is never played for ante and nothing about it is
  recorded.
- **The computer plays combos.** From Magician up, the computer knows the
  classic loops of the card pool: Sliver Queen with Heartstone and
  Ashnod's Altar, Aluren with Man-o'-War and Soul Warden or Pandemonium,
  Enduring Renewal with Ornithopter and Ashnod's Altar or Goblin
  Bombardment, Basalt Monolith with Power Artifact, and Earthcraft with
  the Queen. It puts the pieces together and runs a loop for a reason
  (lethal damage, the mana for a lethal Fireball, a swarm, or life) and
  stops when that is done. At Sorcerer and Wizard it also finds loops of
  its own among the cards it can see. It aims its removal and
  counterspells at the pieces of your loop.
- **No loop can freeze a duel.** A loop that would never end is stopped
  at 1000 steps — spells, abilities and triggers in one step — with a
  message: "You created an infinite loop — stopping at 1000 steps." (or
  the computer's name, when the loop was its). Whatever is already on the
  Spell Chain resolves, and the duel goes on with the next step.
- **Red messages are easier to read.** A refused action or a stopped
  loop is written on the Situation Bar in red with a white outline, so
  it stands out from the bar instead of sinking into it.
- **The computer's turn always ends.** With Aluren on the table, a
  computer holding a Shrieking Drake or a Man-o'-War could cast it for
  free, have it return itself to its hand and cast it again, for ever:
  its turn never ended. It now never repeats a position it has already
  played from, and outside a combo it means to run it never casts one
  card more than eight times in a turn.
- **Creatures that enter with counters are sized right.** The computer
  now judges a Workhorse, a Spike or a Triskelion by the size it will
  have on the table, not by the 0/0 or 1/1 printed on the card, so it
  plays, keeps and brings these creatures back the way their size
  deserves; a Corpse Dance now raises a Workhorse to attack.

## From 0.50.38
- **Your own card back.** Options has a new **Card back** section:
  **Choose...** puts a picture of yours on the back of every card. Use a
  PNG, JPG or WEBP, upright; **456 × 646 pixels** is best, the shape of
  a card. A picture of another shape is trimmed from the middle to fit,
  and a phone photo is turned the way it was held. **Remove** brings the
  usual back again.

## From 0.50.37

- **Cards can be moved right to the edge of the table.** A card you drag
  around the battlefield used to stop short of the edges: a little short
  at the top and sides, and well short of the bottom. Now it goes all the
  way, so its edge touches the edge of the table, on your side and on
  your opponent's. A tapped card parked against an edge stays whole
  while it is turned, and goes back to exactly where you put it when it
  untaps.

## From 0.50.36

- **LAN hosting is calmer under attack.** A LAN connection that sends
  more than 4 KB of junk before joining is let go straight away, even when
  the host's computer is busy. Before, a busy host could print an error
  line every frame for a second and a half before letting it go.

## From 0.50.35

- **The life panel keeps its picture's shape.** The picture behind your
  life total, and the duelist's face the panel turns over to, were
  stretched to fill the panel, which is wider than the 1997 one, so the
  skull, the crystal ball and the green leaf frame came out nearly twice
  as wide as drawn. Now the picture meets the panel halfway: it is
  widened only a little and loses only a little of its top and bottom,
  so it fills the panel and keeps its look.
