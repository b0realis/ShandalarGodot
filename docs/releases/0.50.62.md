# Shandalar 0.50.62

**Magic Public Notation (MPN) — a proposal to the community.** Every duel
you play can now be saved as a public history of the game: what both
players could see, never a hidden hand. MPN is a proposed open format for
such records, independent of this game — reading one needs a JSON parser
and the specification's patch rules, not a rules engine or a card
database. The specification, a player guide and two example files are on
the project's GitHub page, in the `docs` folder (`mpn-specification.md`,
`mpn-v0.1.md`, `mpn-examples`). It is a draft and not an official Magic
standard: makers of engines, viewers, tournament tools and archives are
warmly invited to read it, try it and tell us what it needs, in an issue
on the project's GitHub page with "MPN" in the title.

Fixes on top of 0.50.34, the published release.

- **Move list.** Past games shows any recorded game as a move list, as chess
  games are read: each turn under its player, one action a line under its
  phase (untap, upkeep, draw, main 1, combat, main 2, end), and every symbol
  it uses explained at the top — ⚔ attacks, † died, ✕ exiled, → a target or
  the life left, # the life that ended the game. Copy it, or save it as text.
  The game record stays MPN 0.1; `docs/mpn-move-list.md` defines the move list
  exactly, with two example games, for other Magic programs to show it too.
- A card that leaves play for a hand, a library or nowhere (a token) is named
  in the game record.
- **The creatures roam the land.** The five wizards' creatures walk the
  world as in 1997, on the lands of their own colors, and come after you;
  you see them across the land around you, with their shadows and their
  cries. A kind you have beaten often flees from you.
- **Meeting a creature.** When one reaches you, both antes are shown on the
  1997 meeting screen: duel it, pay it gold, answer its riddle, or change
  your ante with Sleight of Hand; a kind you have beaten five times offers
  tribute instead. Some meetings bring a surprise — a summoned creature,
  First Strike, a card already in play, another deck to play, or Mind
  Control.
- **Duels in the adventure.** Your life is your mana links plus any bonus
  waiting for the next duel; the creatures play their 1997 decks and learn
  your colors. Win and you take cards worth the creature's strength,
  perhaps an amulet, a reward, or a clue to a dungeon instead of the
  cards; lose and your ante is gone; a draw changes nothing. If you quit
  during a duel, you meet the creature again.
- **Lair duels.** In the Abandoned Shrine, the Dragon's Lair, the Hall of
  the Sultan and the Spectral Arena a creature guards a hoard of rare
  cards: duel it, staking nothing, to take the hoard.
- **Sieges and ruled towns.** The wizards attack the towns and cities: a
  news flash names the creature and the town. Beat the creature to save
  the town, or it falls to the wizard with its mana link. Free a ruled town
  by beating its creature. When one wizard rules three towns (five with
  Ring of the Guardian), the Spell of Dominion is cast and the quest has
  failed.
- **Creature quests.** Towns, cities and some villages ask you to defeat a
  creature, for amulets in a village or a card of your choice from a
  town's wares.
- **More of your 1997 game.** The creatures' figures and cries, the meeting
  and reward screens, the news flash and the end come from your copy of
  the 1997 game: build your skin again to bring them in.
- **Copies in your decks, as 1997's deck builder counted them.** A deck may
  hold three copies of a card (four from 59 cards), basic lands aside, and
  Tome of Enlightenment allows one more (any number from 59 cards). A first
  deck dealt with three copies of a spell is no longer called over the
  limit; leaving the deck builder with too many copies asks first, as in
  1997: remove the extras, or go on editing.
- **Three card rules to choose from.** The Embark screen's new Your rules
  offers Adventure limit of cards (as intended in 1997): the shops and the
  creatures' rewards offer no more copies of a card than your deck may
  hold; No limit (as shipped in 1997): any number; and Classic MTG rules:
  at most four copies of a card in a deck, basic lands aside, one of a
  restricted card (four with Tome of Enlightenment) and no banned card —
  and no shop, trader, reward or lair offers you a card you already own as
  many copies of as a deck may hold.
- **Shandalar is still in development.** Shandalar on the main menu first
  says so, and asks you to tell us what you notice — anything that seems
  wrong, breaks, or could be better — as an issue on the project's GitHub
  page; Go test opens the Embark screen, Back stays on the menu.
- On the Embark screen the card packs are greyed for now: each pack's own
  world, ruled by a World Lord, comes in a later version.
- "Ready to Quit?" (Escape or Q on the world) also offers Save and Load.
- **Final battle, a new choice on the Embark screen.** Reload allowed: your
  adventure is saved at Arzakon's door as the final battle begins; lose
  it, and loading puts you back at his door, every battle lost written in
  your record. One life: lose the final battle, or leave or close the game
  during it, and the adventure is over for good; no save of it can fight
  the battle again.
- Importing your 1997 game now also brings the pictures and sounds of the
  dungeons, the castles and the ending, and converts its ending movie
  (when ffmpeg or GStreamer with Theora is installed), ready for when the
  castles and the dungeons open.
- **Fixes across the game.**
  - Your playmat now covers your half of the table in adventure duels.
  - A town you stood in keeps its quest and its visit when you quit and
    resume.
  - Under the Classic MTG rules, every lair's rare obeys the rules too.
  - A freed town shows the card you won, and says when a full bag loses
    it; a reward screen with nothing to say is no longer shown. C reads a
    news flash.
  - The computer waits while you are asked whether to quit or concede.
  - The Spell Chain stays on the screen and clear of the Situation Bar
    however long it grows; the Combat window holds any number of creatures
    a side.
  - A combat skipped because nothing could attack no longer stops in the
    second main phase, and Pause holds the countdown and the coin toss
    before a duel.
  - Original type shows a copy's own printed card. At a private hotseat a
    card exiled face down stays hidden until Show hand, and two-human Test
    hands serve the seat that is deciding, with whole names on its turn
    buttons.
  - The windows between a match's or a gauntlet's duels hold all their
    buttons and take the keyboard, and their duels record their real
    format. Escape (and the Quest's back gesture) closes the title menu's
    popups and leaves Gauntlet Options.
  - A draft pool saved with every card checked takes in the packs you
    enable later.
  - MicroProse 1997 (.dck) exports open and list in the original game, and
    decks from its Playdeck folder import.
  - A full disk no longer leaves a cut-short game record, move list, draft
    or tournament checkpoint behind: the game says the save failed and
    keeps the earlier one.
  - LAN: an open table can no longer be filled by idle connections, and a
    player who finds it full is told so; Export MPN is offered only when
    the host has the record.
  - Web: Choose... for the skin and the card back works again after a file
    box that never answered. Quest and Android: Open Folder and Open save
    folder are greyed, with the reason.
  - A skin chosen again under the same name no longer blanks pictures until
    the restart, and a damaged file of your own no longer floods the log.
  - A creature's reward that carries your lives over starts the next duel at
    exactly the life it names.
  - Pause holds the original coin-toss movie and its result too.
  - A MicroProse .dck export leaves out the cards the original game has no
    number for, and says how many.
- **Rules and cards.**
  - Spells with no life cost can be cast while your life is below zero
    (Lich, and the 1997 rule that checks life only at the end of the phase).
  - "Whenever this deals damage" triggers once for all the damage dealt at
    once: El-Hajjâj, Spirit Link, Zebra Unicorn, Emberwilde Caliph and
    Warrior Angel.
  - Winds of Rath, Extinction, Reign of Terror and twenty more sweepers
    destroy everything at once, so nothing shrinks or grows in between.
  - Whimsy's random effects respect protection and shroud; Glyph of Doom
    works when its Wall dies blocking.
  - Chaoslace and Deathlace beat an earlier color change; a stolen spell
    reflected by Reflect Damage or Eye for an Eye goes back to its caster.
  - Attack taxes such as Propaganda are paid before Leviathan's sacrifice;
    token copies of Firestorm Phoenix and Gravebane Zombie follow their
    text; Sand Golem returns with its counter; a masked creature phasing in
    tapped turns face up; Lich no longer draws when it enters at negative
    life; Sengir Vampire, Axelrod Gunnarson and Brine Hag no longer count a
    creature that left and came back; a stolen Goblin Rock Sled untaps on
    its new controller's turn.
  - Automatic payment with Black Lotus wastes less mana; Spell Blast says
    why it cannot target a spell; the computer no longer peeks under
    Illusionary Mask when it forecasts combat.
- **Dungeons on the land.** A dungeon you hold a clue to now shows on the
  land and on the small map, even where you haven't explored yet; point at
  it to read its name.
- **The wizards' window.** F6, or the hero pane's Wizards tab, opens 1997's
  hall of the five wizards: each wizard's staff drains as you beat his
  creatures, his gems count the towns he rules, skulls count your wins, and
  his life and mana domes are written below; once the castles open, a
  beaten wizard's castle on the Hero page shows his room. The pictures come from your copy of
  the 1997 game: build your skin again to bring them in.
- **The creature record** shows 1997's pictures and colors for each
  creature you have met; its All creatures switch shows the whole list,
  the ones never fought as blank cells.
- **1997's keys on the world.** F6 the wizards, L loads, Escape or Q asks
  "Ready to Quit?" (Y or Yes saves and leaves), Space stops the walk, and a
  right click (or a long press) opens the options where you click. With
  the hero pane open, W, J and D turn to the wizards, the journal, or close
  it. A question's choices answer to their first letter: the letter lights
  a choice, Enter or Space takes it.
- **Places come in as in 1997.** A town's picture zooms in, a castle's and
  a lair's appear tile by tile, a creature's face tiles in — short, skipped
  by any click or key, and switched off with Transitions in Options or in
  the world's options.
- **The first deck as 1997 dealt it:** 36, 40, 46 or 46 cards from
  Apprentice to Wizard, with enchantments as its colored spells.
- Next Duel shows the lives you carry and the lives given apart, as 1997
  did; the city list (F3) also lists the castles you have visited, with
  their wizard, and scrolls smoothly; the map wears its 1997 ornament.
- The quest's words on the meeting's scroll sit in the middle of the
  parchment, as in 1997, smaller when there are many; the purse's numbers
  sit where 1997 put them.
- Your bag holds 500 cards, as in 1997: a shop, a trade or the wise man no
  longer takes your gold or amulets for a card a full bag cannot hold.
- Rare and special cards you get are written in your journal, "Acquired
  spell: …", as in 1997.
- Clicking the town you stand on opens it again without arriving anew: no
  second "Visited" line, and its trade is not drawn again.
- Green creatures no longer reward white cards, and white creatures can
  reward green ones, as in 1997.
- The black wise man's Khabál Ghoul now joins your next duel.
- With the copy limit on, a lair's rare is never a card your deck already
  holds too many of.
- The card trader dims the cards as 1997 did — basic lands never, the cards
  outside the base sets sooner — and shows a count only after the cards
  you own.
- A key pressed just after a window closes no longer reaches the world: an
  extra Escape no longer leaves for the main menu, an extra 1–5 no longer
  casts a World Magic. A double click no longer makes a second choice in a
  town, a ruled town, a news flash or a lair, nor skips a lost card.
- Return to main menu after a duel is over keeps its result: what you won
  or lost is shown, then the adventure is saved. A lair's duel asks its own
  questions: conceding there loses no ante.
- A ruled town's duel no longer plays the town's tune under its own music.
- Closing the game during a news flash no longer saves half a step.
- A full magic bag says what it loses: the cards won, a reward, a lair's
  find; the wise man's copy becomes two extra lives instead, and no card of
  your choice is offered that the bag cannot keep.
- With Big buttons, the meeting and ruled town pictures fill the screen
  again, and keep their size from one step to the next.
- Without the 1997 skin, the meeting's purse says what each number is, the
  creature shows its color and the quest scroll can be read.
- A place's pane always fits the screen with Big buttons.
- Help's deck formats page names the Deck Builder's Restricted and Banned
  filters, which show the restricted and banned cards.

## From 0.50.53

- **Your decks are changed in towns.** As in 1997, the spellbook (F1)
  only shows your decks out in the wilds; a town's Edit deck/Sell cards
  changes them, and so does Conjurer's Will.
- **What a card is worth.** In the adventure's deck builder, the strip
  where other decks keep a sideboard tells what the card under the mouse
  sells for: in a town what that place pays, out in the wilds the least
  and the most the places pay, the best price among the places you have
  been to, and how many you own, in this deck and to sell.
- **Quests.** A village's wise man may give you a quest — Begin a Quest:
  take a letter to the keeper of a town or city some way off, for a mana
  link or an amulet, or bring a card of a color and kind for a mana link
  and an amulet. Four or five days to do it, the quest pane counting them
  on the 1997 clock; arrive in time and the reward is yours, run out of
  time and that town's wise man turns you away for good.
- **The wise man.** Speak to Wise Man: a tale of long ago, news of the
  wizards and the World Magics, a reminder of your quest, or a gift — by
  where his town stands, one of six: a tale, a clue to a castle or
  dungeon, two extra lives in your next duel, a look at the deck of any
  creature of the town's color, a companion card in play in your next
  duel, or a copy of any card you own for three amulets. The tales and the
  news come from your copy of the 1997 game.
- **World Magics for sale.** As you leave a town or city, the nearest World
  Magic you do not own is offered on the 1997 seller's table: where to find
  it, or, in its own town, for its price (100 to 750 gold).
- **Your mana links.** You begin with ten, as in 1997: the ankh counts the
  towns and cities linked to you, each a life in your duels. A letter
  quest makes another.
- **The city list (F3).** Every town and city you have visited, on its
  1997 screen: its colors of land, the cards it trades, its World Magic
  and your mana links.
- **The map, as in 1997 (F2).** The land you have explored on the 1997
  map's stone, with the Main Map (the places named, white where linked,
  yellow where not, a wizard's color for his towns), the Info Map (each
  one's colors, trades and World Magic) and the Plain Map; City Info opens
  the city list. Your journal can be walked on it, each line where it was
  written.
- **Ten named save slots.** Save offers ten slots for each adventure; name
  one and press Enter. The load screen lists each adventure with its
  autosave and its slots; Resume takes up the newest autosave. The
  autosave is written as you leave a town or a lair and as you quit.
- **The world's sounds.** Footsteps on the color of the land, the land's
  birds and beasts, each town's and castle's own tune rising as you come
  near, the panel's clicks and the World Magics' stings — the 1997
  soundscape, with the title's tune soft beneath it.
- **The Guardian's Ghost** shows, for an amulet, the deck of any creature
  of the lair's color.
- **The quest pane's clock.** The 1997 pendulum clock, sun and moon and the
  days left beside your quest, and the next duel's gifts as their icons.
- **Price tags by rarity.** In the card shop a card's price turns black on
  white, silver, gold or purple — its rarity — under the pointer and when
  chosen.
- **New places come into view** block by block, as in 1997, when an
  adventure begins and after Leap of Fate or Sword of Resistance.
- **A steadier adventure.**
  - A place is the one thing you are doing: while a town, lair, shop,
    trader, wise man, World Magic seller, city list or the saves are open,
    the panel still shows your gold, food and amulets but its buttons
    wait, and no click walks your hero. While the map is open only its
    own keys, Escape and Ctrl+S work.
  - Edit deck/Sell cards brings you back to the town when you are done.
  - A lair is spent the moment you enter it; quitting in the middle of a
    lair loses the card still waiting there. The nearby town's tune falls
    silent inside.
  - A card trade checks your amulets and gold again when you accept, and
    the Guardian's Ghost will not take an amulet you no longer hold.
  - A village whose quest you failed offers no other quest; its wise man
    turns you away.
  - Stepping straight back into the town you just left neither restocks
    its shop nor gives a new card hint, as in 1997. With your copy of the
    1997 game a shop may sell Mishra's Factory or Strip Mine, as its shops
    did, and the Nomad's Bazaar asks half again for a colorless card, as a
    town does.
  - On the city list and the Info Map a town no longer shows a World Magic
    it does not sell.
  - M on the Main Map closes the map; Previous and the wheel stop at the
    journal's first line.
  - With the hero's pane open, the journal and the creature record keep
    their place while you walk, and a long journal opens at once. The
    journal keeps up to 10,000 lines, five times as many as in 1997.
  - In the card trader, Escape in the name box clears the name first.
  - A place's picture shrinks so its pane always fits the screen with Big
    buttons.
  - The name of the place under the pointer follows the land as you walk,
    look or zoom; a quick double click on a pane's Done no longer sends
    your hero walking. A click while the hero glides lands on the tile
    you see under the pointer, and a walk that ends in a town or lair no
    longer leaves the place's name showing beside its pane.
  - In a town's Edit deck/Sell cards, Undo and Restore deck no longer put
    back a card you have sold, and the Sell button follows the deck as you
    change it. The adventure's decks keep no choice of artwork, so the
    artwork picker is gone from their builder.
  - Two quick Escapes on the Load list close only the list.
  - The small map no longer shows black notches along its left edge while
    you walk.
  - Without the original pictures, the amulet counts on the map and the
    city list keep their colors.
  - After Leap of Fate or Sword of Resistance the old view dissolves in
    its right place at any window size.
  - Turning a card pack on or off mid-game, or adding a skin, updates the
    shops, trades, riddles, hints and the wise man's words at once.
- **Your saves are safe.** A save is written whole or not at all: a full
  disk or a failed write keeps your last good save, and the game says the
  save failed — Exit game asks before leaving without saving, and a new
  adventure's arrival says so if its first save failed. Each save keeps
  its previous version beside it, taken up if the save itself is damaged.
  One damaged save no longer hides the others: the list shows every save
  it can read and says how many it could not, and a damaged slot reads
  "(Cannot be read)" rather than "(Empty)". Saves made before the ten
  slots move into the first free slot. Save times are shown on your own
  clock, and the autosave no longer carries a slot's name.
- **Smoother.** The hero and the view glide between steps instead of
  moving in small jumps. Nothing on the world is redrawn while the hero
  stands still, and walking, looking around and zooming only move the
  picture, so the world stays smooth zoomed out and on handhelds; the
  small map and the open map no longer keep the processor busy, and the
  world's pointers are ready at once. Entering a town, buying a card and
  opening the save and load lists no longer pause, even with a large
  collection or many saves, and the adventure's deck builder (F1, the
  spellbook, Edit deck/Sell cards) opens much faster — the first time of
  a session too, as your cards' pictures are prepared in the background a
  moment after the world opens.
- **The deck builders open faster.** The ordinary deck builder and Booster
  Draft's open several times faster after the first time, and with card
  packs enabled the filters answer about three times faster.
- **A hero could get stuck on the coast:** a click or a save while
  crossing the corner of a sea tile left them unable to move. Fixed, and a
  game saved that way loads with the hero on land. The small map no longer
  shows dark notches along its left edge.

## From 0.50.52

- **The card shop.** In a village, town or city, Buy Cards opens its shop
  on the 1997 shop's own ground, "Cards for Sale": four cards in a
  village, five in a town, six in a city (one more with Haggler's Coin),
  drawn as the 1997 game drew them — the colors of the land the town
  stands on, a basic land among them, common, uncommon and rare places in
  turn, never more copies than your deck may hold — and kept: a card you
  buy leaves its place empty until a new one comes, days later. Rest the
  pointer on a card to see it large, click it, Buy. Your adventure keeps
  every town's shop.
- **Sell your cards.** The deck builder opened from a town's Edit
  deck/Sell cards has Sell: one copy of the card on show, never one the
  deck on the surface holds, for the town's price.
- **The 1997 prices.** A card's price is the 1997 game's own formula — its
  type, cost, power and toughness, keywords and abilities, its rarity —
  three times in a village, four in a town, five in a city, more for a
  card of another color. Build your skin again with
  `tools/import_original.py` and the prices are exactly the 1997 game's,
  read from your copy of it (every card it lists, however it spells the
  name); without it, and for every card the 1997 game never had, the same
  formula reads the card itself, its abilities included.
- **Trade for cards.** Every town and city trades one kind of card — a
  color's, or a type's, such as any creatures — for amulets of a color of
  its land: "Trade red amulet for Any creatures" stands where the wise man
  is while you hold one. The trader lists every card of that kind with
  how many you own, with color and type chips and a name to look for; a
  card costs its rarity in amulets, basic lands four for one. After a
  trade the town waits some days before the next.
- **The card seller's hints.** "Buy Cards/Card Hints": the seller points
  out a card on offer that works with one you own, and what the two do
  together, in the 1997 game's words from your copy of it.
- **Lairs.** Lairs lie hidden about the land and show, in the 1997 game's
  own pictures, only when you come beside one: walk onto it and it opens.
  Two times in three it holds a card of its color; otherwise one of
  fourteen places — a Ruined Tower's clue, riddles on cards at
  Loremaster's Tower and the Pyramids of Aska-tet, the Thieves Hideout,
  the Lost City of El'Akram, the Gemcutter's Guild's amulets, the Nomad's
  Bazaar (any card for gold), the Diamond Mine (any card for amulets) and
  the Oasis of Muldoon (a card for five extra life in your next duel). The
  lairs that hold duels wait for the duels. Sometimes a rare waits as
  well, given as you leave.
- **Read a card full screen.** Right-click a card in a shop, the card
  trader or a lair (on a touch screen, press and hold) and it opens full
  screen, as in a duel. The options switch it: "Large card on
  right-click".

## From 0.50.51

- **Cast the World Magics.** On the world map, click a World Magic's icon
  or press its number to cast it: Conjurer's Will (1), Leap of Fate (2),
  Quickening (3), Staff of Thunder (4) and Sword of Resistance (5), the
  number shown on the icon while it can be cast. Leap of Fate sets you
  down somewhere else in Shandalar, wherever the dice say; Quickening gives
  a burst of speed, its icon lit while it lasts. As in 1997, each risks an
  amulet of its color: an Apprentice loses it one time in four, a Wizard
  every time. A World Magic with nothing to work on says so and risks
  nothing. Each icon's tip now gives its 1997 words.
- **Panes you can move.** A small map in the corner shows the land round
  your hero (N shows or hides it; click on it to walk there). A quest pane
  shows what calls you, in the 1997 game's words: the quest, where to go
  and the days left, a city under siege, a bonus for your next duel and
  the cities each wizard has tapped toward the Spell of Dominion. Guide
  arrows at the screen's edge point to the quest's town and to a city under
  siege. The gate on the panel (F4) opens the dungeon and castle clues:
  treasure, creatures, rules and where each lies. The small map, the quest
  pane, the clues, your hero's journal and the keys float in panes with
  round corners; drag one anywhere and it stays there, next time too.
  Quests, sieges and clues are found once the towns and the encounters
  come.
- **Your first deck and the deck builder.** A new hero sets out with a
  first deck dealt by the 1997 game's rules: by difficulty, your color's
  basic lands, spells and creatures and one rare, then more colors, from
  the base cards at the rarity of their first printing — 36 cards for an
  Apprentice up to 45 for a Wizard. A town's Edit deck/Sell cards opens
  the deck builder over the world: the cards along the bottom are your
  own, those not yet in the deck on show, and no deck holds more copies
  than you own.
  Deck1, Deck2 and Deck3 are your three decks; the one on show when you
  press Done is the deck you duel with. The deck's name pane shows your
  gold, "GOLD: 200", as the 1997 builder's title did; click the name to
  rename the deck, and your adventure keeps it. Nothing from outside
  your cards: no Extras, loading, proxies, drafts or sideboard; Tome of
  Enlightenment eases the duplicate limit. Conjurer's Will opens it too,
  and the spellbook (F1) opens it to look.
- **Your hero's stats.** The faces (F5) open a pane of your hero's stats
  in the 1997 stats screen's own captions: your name, what you carry, your World Magics, the wizards' castles conquered and the
  attempts at each, cities saved and recovered, the quest record, duels
  won and lost, dungeon treasures, your record against each creature and
  your days on the road; a Wizards tab with each wizard's cities tapped
  toward Dominion, and the Journal, newest first.
- **Touch and touchpads.** On the world map a pinch zooms, on a
  touchscreen or a laptop's touchpad, and a touchpad's two-finger slide
  looks around. Big buttons, on by themselves where the touch controls
  are on and switchable in Options, make the panel and its options large
  enough for a finger; the panel's row scrolls with a swipe or with red
  arrows at its two ends. The world's screens were checked from 640 × 400
  up to 3440 × 1440, Steam Deck's 1280 × 800 included, and in a tall
  window; the quest pane now moves out of the way when the panel goes to
  the bottom. A long journal scrolls, with a gold bar.
- **Visit the places of Shandalar.** Walk to a village, town or city, a
  wizard's castle, a lair or a dungeon you have clues to, and it opens in
  a pane in the middle of the screen with the 1997 game's own picture of
  the place — the village's farmhouse, the city at dusk, each wizard's
  castle, the lairs (the blue one's sunken ship) — its name and the 1997
  words and choices. In a town you can open your deck and buy 10 food
  (15, 20 or 25 gold, as in 1997); a castle asks whether you dare to
  enter. The card shop, the wise man, the duels and the dungeons come
  next. Build your skin again with `tools/import_original.py` to bring the

## From 0.50.39

- **Test hands.** Magic Battle has a new **Test hands** button, at the
  bottom of its settings under the ante and match settings. Choose
  the decks and the opponent as usual, then set up each side: the cards in
  hand (picked from the deck, dealt at random, or any card from your
  enabled packs), the cards on the table (tapped or untapped, and a
  creature summoning sick or ready), the graveyard, the top of the library

  classic loops of the card pool: Sliver Queen with Heartstone and
  Ashnod's Altar, Aluren with Man-o'-War and Soul Warden or Pandemonium,
  Enduring Renewal with Ornithopter and Ashnod's Altar or Goblin
  Bombardment, Basalt Monolith with Power Artifact, and Earthcraft with
  the Queen. It puts the pieces together and runs a loop for a reason
  (lethal damage, the mana for a lethal Fireball, a swarm, or life) and
