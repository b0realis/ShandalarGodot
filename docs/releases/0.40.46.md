# Shandalar 0.40.46

The second Steam Deck playtest report, answered: a graveyard that
flashed, a big card over the windows, and a D-pad that lost the hand.

- **The D-pad walks the hand.** A hop lands on the part of a card that
  is actually showing — in the hand window every card but the last
  shows only the band of its top edge under the next, and the old hop
  aimed at each card's centre, found the next card there and gave up
  (*"left does not work"*). Up and down now walk the stack one card at
  a time, left and right leave it for what stands beside it, a hop
  never lands on what is already under the pointer, and the graveyard
  and exile plates are hop targets like any button (`PadControls._aim`,
  `pad_target`).
- **One press is one press.** Steam Input can send a game two copies of
  a gesture — the first playtest's layout sent Escape beside pad B; a
  layout that sends a mouse click beside A opened a pile with one copy
  and closed it with the other. The pad pointer treats a real mouse
  press within 100 ms of its own press of that button (either order) as
  the same press, a second controller reporting a button already down
  is not a second press, and the graveyard/exile view ignores a click on
  its dim for a quarter of a second after it opens (`GraveyardView.SETTLE_MS`).
- **The big card is a panel, not a popup.** The docked big card in the
  sidebar sat at the examine popup's height, over the combat window
  dragged across it, the hand window and a card in flight. Docked it
  now rests at its screen's own level, under every window; undocked it
  is still the popup that draws over everything (`CardPreview.docked`,
  `POPUP_Z`). While a pile is open the duel lifts it over the view's
  dim — the dim would darken the very card the pile fills — and rests
  it again when the view closes.
- `docs/handhelds.md` describes the stack walk, the plates as targets
  and the doubled-press guards.
- No wire change: protocol 24 peers interoperate.
