# Issues #7–#10 — a player's reports of 2026-11-07, fixed 2026-11-10

Four reports on GitHub from one player, made on 0.60.18; the owner: *"Check,
study and fix and document issues #7 - #10 (#11 ignore as it is feature
requests for the future)"*. Each fix carries a test that failed on the code
before it. The fixes ship in 0.60.19.

## #7 — mana from dual lands and Birds of Paradise: choose the color needed

**Report.** Casting Whirling Dervish ({G}{G}), Pendelhaven tapped for {G},
then Savannah clicked: the game asked Green or White. *"I think it should
automatically select what I need."* Birds of Paradise asked the same.

**Cause.** While a cast or activation waits for its mana, a source with more
than one mana ability (a dual land has one per color) always opened the
color menu, and an "any color" ability always asked.

**Fix.** `ManaPlanner.payment_pick()` (`engine/mana_planner.gd`) chooses,
for a click made while paying, the one ability that pays a symbol still owed
and leaves the rest of the cost payable from the other untapped sources; with
only generic mana owed, the first color, unless that would leave extra mana
whose color is the player's to name. Options that differ beyond color (a
painland's painless {C} and painful {W}) are picked only when exactly one
pays, and never one that sacrifices its source. The menu or the question
stays when the choice is really the player's: two owed colors both workable,
a color-choice ability (Fellwar Stone, Reflecting Pool — the owner's ruling
of 2026-09-17 that it asks), a converter, restricted mana, Celestial Dawn.
`DuelScreen._tap_for_payment` and the LAN guest's
`SgDuelView._tap_for_payment` use it. A click outside a payment keeps the
menu, so a dual land still floats the color the player picks. No new
setting; the 1997 "Don't auto tap this card" mark is for the double-click.

**Tests.** `tests/ui/test_issue_7_payment_color.gd`,
`tests/ui/test_issue_7_lan_guest.gd`, `tests/unit/test_issue_7_payment_pick.gd`;
`tests/ui/test_campaign_fix_ui_announcement.gd` reached City of Brass's menu
to test the announcement bracket and now pays directly (the menu path is
pinned by `test_the_menus_choice_opens_the_announcement_bracket_too`).

## #8 — the tap prompt should show the mana still owed

**Report.** The bar read "Tap Swords to Plowshares".

**Already fixed** in 0.60.19 before the report reached us (c3b9d56): the
bar reads "CASTING: Swords to Plowshares, Tap {W}" as 1997 did and counts
the mana down as it is paid ("Tap {3}{W}{W}" → "Tap {3}{W}" after a Plains),
pinned by `tests/ui/test_casting_flow.gd`. The report's second half —
choosing the color for you — is #7.

## #9 — Magical Hack on a dual land: what should the card show?

**Report.** A Savannah hacked Forest → Island: the band read "Island/Plains"
and taps gave {U} or {W}, right — but the big card and the tooltip still
said "({T}: Add {G} or {W}.)".

**Cause.** The Showcase (`CardPreview`) and the tooltip read the printed
text; a text change rewrote only the engine's live characteristics.

**Fix.** `CardPreview.changed_text(inst)` replays the engine's own layer-3
pass (CR 612) on a scratch copy of the printed card and rewrites only what
that pass changed — land types in the type line, landwalk and its reminder,
the color after "protection from", the mana symbols after "Add" (checked
against the recomputed mana abilities, so a Blood Moon, which is no text
change, never shows here) — with the changed words in italics
(`ManaText` draws italic ranges, a slanted mana symbol leaning). A LAN
guest's card reads the host's text effects. The tooltip shows "Rules
(changed by a text change):" over "Printed rules:". "Original type" still
shows the printed card. The 1997 manual (p.118) has the Showcase show the
original text; the change is a convenience the report asked for.

**Tests.** `tests/ui/test_issue_9_text_change_text.gd`.

## #10 — the dungeon scroll's card is too small to read

**Report.** "Correct!" showed the riddle's card unreadably small at a high
resolution.

**Cause.** `DungeonPane._show_box` drew the card 96 points wide times the
page factor — 120×171 px at every resolution, since the page is one 1422×800
layout scaled.

**Fix.** The box shows the Showcase's own card as tall as the page leaves
room for, at most its full 300×428 and never smaller than before, refitted
when the window changes; the creature meeting's riddle card goes from 150 to
210 page points beside the portrait; a left click on either opens the card
reader, as a right click already did. The lair's riddle card was already
large (259×370).

**Tests.** `tests/ui/test_issue_10_riddle_card_size.gd`.
